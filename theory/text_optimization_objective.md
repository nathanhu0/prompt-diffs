# Text Optimization Objective — Derivations

> Working notes. Every displayed equation is numbered. Claims are not
> treated as proven until marked ∎. Assumptions are stated explicitly and
> referenced by number. Nothing here is asserted unless it traces to a
> stated definition, assumption, or prior line.

## Notation and conventions

- $M$ — a fixed (base) language model. It induces a conditional
  distribution over responses given a query and a system prompt.
- $p(y \mid x, \pi)$ — the probability $M$ assigns to response
  $y \in \mathcal{Y}$ given query $x \in \mathcal{X}$ and system prompt
  $\pi$. (Here $y$ is a full response; for a token sequence
  $y = (y_1,\dots,y_T)$ this is the product of per-token conditionals.)
- $\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{Y}$ — query / response spaces (token sequences).
- $Q$ — a distribution over queries $x$.
- $\Pi$ — the feasible set of candidate system prompts (the optimization
  domain).
- $\pi^\star \in \Pi$ — the canonical ("ground-truth") system prompt used
  to generate the data. The superscript $\star$ denotes the *target
  generator*; that it is also the optimizer of $\mathcal{L}$ is **earned**
  by Claim 1, not assumed by the notation.
- $p_\pi(\cdot \mid x) := p(\cdot \mid x, \pi)$ — shorthand for the
  conditional response distribution induced by prompt $\pi$ at query $x$.
  Lets us write identifiability as injectivity of the map
  $\pi \mapsto p_\pi$.

## Setup

Throughout, responses are token sequences of bounded length, so
$\mathcal{Y}$ is finite and every entropy and expectation below is a finite
number.

**Definition 1 (Data-generating process).** Data is generated by context
distillation from $\pi^\star$: sample a query $x \sim Q$, then sample a
response $y \sim p(\cdot \mid x, \pi^\star)$. The induced joint
distribution over $(x,y)$ is
$$ \mathcal{D}(x,y) \;=\; Q(x)\, p(y \mid x, \pi^\star). \tag{1} $$

*Remark.* This is **context distillation** in general. It is
**subliminal learning** in the regime where $\pi^\star$ and the support of
$Q$ are semantically unrelated (the system prompt's "trait" is not
elicited by, or about, the queries).

**Definition 2 (Population NLL objective).** Text optimization seeks a
prompt $\pi \in \Pi$ minimizing the expected negative log-likelihood of
the generated data:
$$ \mathcal{L}(\pi) \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{x \sim Q}\,
   \mathbb{E}_{y \sim p(\cdot \mid x, \pi^\star)}
   \big[ -\log p(y \mid x, \pi) \big]. \tag{2} $$
Minimizing $\mathcal{L}$ is equivalent to maximizing the expected
log-likelihood the candidate prompt assigns to data drawn from $\pi^\star$.

**Claim 1 (to be proven).** $\pi^\star$ is a minimizer of $\mathcal{L}$
over $\Pi$. Moreover, under an identifiability condition (to be stated),
it is the *unique* minimizer.

## Proof of Claim 1

**Lemma 1 (Excess loss is expected forward KL).** For every $\pi\in\Pi$,
$$ \mathcal{L}(\pi) - \mathcal{L}(\pi^\star)
   \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{x \sim Q}\Big[\,
   D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\big(p_{\pi^\star}(\cdot\mid x)\,\big\|\,p_\pi(\cdot\mid x)\big)
   \Big]. \tag{3} $$

*Proof.* Both losses average over the same samples $(x,y)\sim\mathcal{D}$
and differ only in which prompt scores $y$. Subtracting them:
$$ \mathcal{L}(\pi) - \mathcal{L}(\pi^\star)
   = \mathbb{E}_{x\sim Q}\,\mathbb{E}_{y\sim p_{\pi^\star}(\cdot\mid x)}
     \Big[\,-\log p_\pi(y\mid x) \;-\; \big(-\log p_{\pi^\star}(y\mid x)\big)\Big]. \tag{3a} $$
The two logs combine into $\log\frac{p_{\pi^\star}(y\mid x)}{p_\pi(y\mid x)}$:
$$ = \mathbb{E}_{x\sim Q}\,\mathbb{E}_{y\sim p_{\pi^\star}(\cdot\mid x)}
     \left[\, \log \frac{p_{\pi^\star}(y\mid x)}{p_\pi(y\mid x)} \,\right]. \tag{3b} $$
For each fixed $x$ this inner average is exactly
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(p_{\pi^\star}(\cdot\mid x)\,\|\,p_\pi(\cdot\mid x)\big)$
(the definition of KL, $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|q)=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim p}[\log\frac{p(y)}{q(y)}]$).
The outer $\mathbb{E}_{x\sim Q}$ gives (3). $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Theorem 1 ($\pi^\star$ minimizes; the minimizer set).** $\mathcal{L}(\pi)\ge
\mathcal{L}(\pi^\star)$ for every $\pi\in\Pi$, so $\pi^\star\in\arg\min_{\pi\in
\Pi}\mathcal{L}$. Moreover
$$ \arg\min_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{L} = \big\{\pi\in\Pi : p_\pi(\cdot\mid x)=
   p_{\pi^\star}(\cdot\mid x)\ \text{for } Q\text{-a.e. } x\big\}. \tag{4} $$

*Proof.* By Lemma 1, $\mathcal{L}(\pi)-\mathcal{L}(\pi^\star)=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim
Q}\big[D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{\pi^\star}(\cdot\mid x)\,\|\,p_\pi(\cdot\mid x))\big]$.
Each summand is $\ge0$ (Gibbs), so the expectation is $\ge0$ and
$\mathcal{L}(\pi)\ge\mathcal{L}(\pi^\star)$; equality holds at $\pi^\star$ since
its KL term is identically $0$. For general $\pi$, $\mathcal{L}(\pi)=\mathcal{L}
(\pi^\star)$ iff the nonnegative integrand vanishes $Q$-a.e., i.e. (strict
Gibbs, $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|q)=0\iff p=q$) iff $p_\pi(\cdot\mid x)=p_{\pi^\star}
(\cdot\mid x)$ for $Q$-a.e. $x$ — which is (4). $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Definition 3 (Behavioral equivalence).** Write $\pi\sim\pi'\iff p_\pi(\cdot\mid
x)=p_{\pi'}(\cdot\mid x)$ for $Q$-a.e. $x$. Then (4) says exactly $\arg\min_{\pi
\in\Pi}\mathcal{L}=\Pi\cap[\pi^\star]_\sim$: the minimizers are precisely the
prompts behaviorally indistinguishable from $\pi^\star$ under $Q$. So
**uniqueness of the minimizer $\iff$ $[\pi^\star]_\sim=\{\pi^\star\}$** — which
the next section establishes.

## Uniqueness: the prompt $\to$ distribution map is injective

By Theorem 1 and Def. 3, $\pi^\star$ is the unique minimizer iff distinct
prompts induce distinct response distributions under $Q$. We obtain this by
extending the injectivity theorem of Nikolaou et al. (2025), *Language Models
are Injective and Hence Invertible* (arXiv:2510.15511), from hidden states to
output distributions.

**Model notation.** Let $\theta$ be the model parameters, $r(s;\theta)\in
\mathbb{R}^d$ the last-token hidden state for input sequence $s$, $\mathrm{N}$
the final normalization (RMSNorm/LayerNorm), $U\in\mathbb{R}^{|V|\times d}$ the
unembedding, and
$$ f(s;\theta)=\mathrm{softmax}\big(U\,\mathrm{N}(r(s;\theta))\big)\in
   \Delta^{|V|-1} \tag{5} $$
the next-token distribution. The response distribution $p_\pi(\cdot\mid x)$ of
Def. 2 has first-token marginal $f((\pi,x);\theta)$.

**Cited (representation-level injectivity).** Nikolaou et al. prove $s\mapsto
r(s;\theta_T)$ is injective a.s. over initialization and through any finite
number of GD steps. Structure: for fixed $s\ne s'$, $h(\theta)=\|r(s;\theta)-
r(s';\theta)\|^2$ is real-analytic in $\theta$ (Thm 2.1 / Prop B.3); by the
real-analytic zero-set dichotomy (Thm A.1, after Mityagin) its zero set is
Lebesgue-null unless $h\equiv0$; a witness $\theta^\star$ with $h(\theta^\star)>
0$ (Thm C.2, Cases A/B) rules out $h\equiv0$; density-based initialization
(Thm 2.2), a union bound over the finite prompt set $\mathcal{V}^{\le K}$, and
GD-preservation of absolute continuity of the parameter law (Thm 2.3 /
Cor C.5.1) make injectivity hold simultaneously over all pairs, a.s., through
training. *(Theorem labels as cited from the paper / our prior writeup.)*

**Proposition B (distribution-level injectivity).** Under the same hypotheses,
a.s. over initialization and through finite training,
$$ s\ne s'\ \Longrightarrow\ f(s;\theta)\ne f(s';\theta). \tag{6} $$

*Proof.* The paper also proves the readout $h\mapsto\mathrm{softmax}(U\,
\mathrm{N}(h))$ is real-analytic (Prop B.3(3)); hence $f(\cdot;\theta)$, and so
the output-distribution gap
$$ \tilde h(\theta)=\|f(s;\theta)-f(s';\theta)\|^2, \tag{7} $$
are real-analytic in $\theta$. The dichotomy, union bound, density init, and
GD-preservation above refer only to *an analytic function of $\theta$ and the
parameter measure* — never to what it measures — so they transfer verbatim with
$\tilde h$ in place of $h$. Only the witness must be strengthened: we need
$\theta^\star$ with $\tilde h(\theta^\star)>0$.

Take the Thm C.2 construction, which gives $r(s;\theta^\star)\ne r(s';
\theta^\star)$, and make two generic choices on top of it:

1. **(distinct after normalization)** choose the construction's free embedding
   rows so that $\boldsymbol\delta:=\mathrm{N}(r(s;\theta^\star))-\mathrm{N}(r(s';
   \theta^\star))\ne\mathbf 0$. For RMSNorm with $\epsilon>0$ this is *automatic*
   — $\mathrm{N}$ is then injective (a radial bijection), so $r(s)\ne r(s')
   \Rightarrow\boldsymbol\delta\ne\mathbf 0$. For LayerNorm it excludes the
   measure-zero set where the two states are related by LN's mean-shift/scaling
   invariance.
2. **(unembedding preserves the difference)** with $\mathrm{N}$-gain $\gamma=
   \mathbf 1$ and bias $\beta=\mathbf 0$, choose $U$ so that $U\boldsymbol\delta
   \notin\mathrm{span}(\mathbf 1)$.

The logit gap is then $U\,\mathrm{N}(r(s;\theta^\star))-U\,\mathrm{N}(r(s';
\theta^\star))=U\boldsymbol\delta$ ($\beta$ cancels in the difference), and since
$\mathrm{softmax}(\ell)=\mathrm{softmax}(\ell')\iff\ell-\ell'\in\mathrm{span}
(\mathbf 1)$, choice 2 gives $f(s;\theta^\star)\ne f(s';\theta^\star)$, i.e.
$\tilde h(\theta^\star)>0$. Both choices are realizable: once $\boldsymbol\delta
\ne\mathbf 0$, the set $\{U:U\boldsymbol\delta\in\mathrm{span}(\mathbf 1)\}$ is
the preimage of a line under the linear map $U\mapsto U\boldsymbol\delta$ — a
proper subspace — so almost every $U$ works. Thus $\tilde h\not\equiv0$, and the
paper's apparatus delivers (6). $\qquad\blacksquare$

*Remark (the one paper-dependent line).* Choice 1 needs the C.2 construction to
leave the relevant embedding rows free enough to force $\boldsymbol\delta\ne
\mathbf 0$; this should be read off the actual C.2 statement. It is moot for our
models, which use RMSNorm ($\epsilon>0$): there choice 1 is automatic and only
choice 2 remains.

*Remark (a deterministic sufficient condition).* The a.s. qualifier on choice 2
is removable when $\mathbf 1\notin\mathrm{col}(U)$ (the model cannot emit a
uniform logit shift): then $U\boldsymbol\delta\in\mathrm{span}(\mathbf 1)$ forces
$\boldsymbol\delta=\mathbf 0$ for full-column-rank $U$, so the readout is
injective *deterministically* on top of the paper's representation-injectivity.
Since $\mathrm{col}(U)$ is a $d$-dim subspace of $\mathbb{R}^{|V|}$ with $d\ll
|V|$, $\mathbf 1\notin\mathrm{col}(U)$ holds generically and is checkable via
$\min_v\|Uv-\mathbf 1\|>0$.

**Theorem 2 (Exact uniqueness).** A.s. over initialization and through finite
training: for every query $x$ and every $\pi\ne\pi^\star$, $p_\pi(\cdot\mid x)
\ne p_{\pi^\star}(\cdot\mid x)$. Hence $[\pi^\star]_\sim=\{\pi^\star\}$ and, by
Theorem 1, $\pi^\star$ is the **unique** minimizer of $\mathcal{L}$.

*Proof.* Apply Proposition B to each pair $s=(\pi,x)$, $s'=(\pi^\star,x)$
(distinct, since $\pi\ne\pi^\star$); these are among the finitely many pairs
covered by the union bound, so $f((\pi,x);\theta)\ne f((\pi^\star,x);\theta)$
for all $x,\pi$ simultaneously a.s. Distinct first-token marginals imply
distinct response distributions, so no $\pi\ne\pi^\star$ lies in $[\pi^\star]_
\sim$; Theorem 1 forces $\arg\min=\{\pi^\star\}$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

*Remark (exact vs. statistical identifiability).* Theorem 2 is an *exact,
population* statement, a.s. in weight space. It coexists with practical
near-degeneracy: a paraphrase $\pi'$ of $\pi^\star$ has $p_{\pi'}\ne p_{\pi^
\star}$ but excess loss $\mathcal{L}(\pi')-\mathcal{L}(\pi^\star)=\mathbb{E}_x
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{\pi^\star}\|p_{\pi'})\approx0$. Finite samples and finite
optimization cannot resolve $\pi^\star$ from this cloud — the obstruction to
recovery is statistical/numerical, not the existence of exact ties.
